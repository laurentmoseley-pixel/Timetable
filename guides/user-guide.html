Timetable Builder: user guide

This guide is for the people who build the timetable and run the day: timetablers, senior leaders, and office staff who arrange cover. The same help is inside the app: choose Help in the status bar at the bottom, or press Ctrl K and type help.

The screen

  • Home is the page the app opens on. To do lists what needs you today, each with one button that goes straight to the job: lessons nobody covers (Find cover), clashes (Show me), lessons still to place (Place them), staff below your minimums, unsaved work and backups. Below it are the headline numbers (staff away, cover, problems, teaching load, rooms in use), safety and staffing through the day, cover so far this term, and quick jobs. Home only shows things; nothing on it changes the timetable.

  • The menu on the left:

    • Home
    • Plan: Timetable (was Build), Auto build, What if (was Scenarios), Insights
    • Run the day: Staff day, Today, Cover
    • Look up: People and rooms (Staff, Students and Rooms, with a switch at the top), Reports
    • More (click to open; it stays open until you close it): Subject choices (was Options), Rest days and on call (was Rota), Transport, One person (was Individual)
    • Set up: Setup

    Search (Ctrl K) still finds pages by their old names.

  • Take the tour (on Home, or File, Help) shows where everything is in seven short steps. Escape ends it.

  • After each save, the status at the top shows a Saved tick for a moment. Hover over Save, Undo or Search to see their keyboard shortcuts; press ? for the full list. On a phone, the menu is a bar along the bottom.

  • The header shows:

    • the page you are on
    • search (Ctrl K), to jump to any page, person, class or job
    • where the timetable is kept
    • the bell, for telling people about changes
    • Undo and Redo, Save, and the File menu

  • The status bar at the bottom shows:

    • whether your work is saved
    • any problems to put right, and the quality score (click it for Checks)
    • how big the timetable is
    • Help

  • Appearance: File menu, then Light, Dark, High contrast or Automatic. There is also an easier-to-read font.

  • Look (trial): File menu, then Classic or Future. Future is a new look: glass panels, a soft background light, glowing highlights and monospace times. It changes how the app looks, not what it does. It works with Light, Dark and Automatic; High contrast always uses the plain style, and so do printouts. Reduce effects (next to it) turns off the glass, glow and movement. Your choice is remembered on this computer.

Starting

A new, empty timetable opens with a welcome screen:

  • Look around with a demo school. A made-up school, to try everything. Choose Start my own timetable when you are ready.
  • Set up my school, step by step. Name the school and timetable, choose the school days, and set how many lessons there are, how long they are, and when break and lunch fall.
  • Bring in what we have. An Excel workbook, the Excel template filled in, TimeTabler exports, or a backup file.

Then, in Setup, add staff, pupils, groups (classes and sets), subjects and rooms. You can type them, paste a list, or bring in the CSV templates. The Getting started list on the Today page shows what is left to do.

Building the timetable

  • Timetable shows one row per group and one column per period. Click a slot to add or change a lesson in the editor on the right.

  • Drag a lesson to move it. Hold Ctrl to copy it. While you drag, the group's row shows:

    • green where it fits
    • amber where it fits but replaces a lesson or repeats the subject that day
    • red where it would break a rule

    If you drop it where it breaks a rule, the app asks first.

  • Alt and the arrow keys move the chosen lesson a period or a day at a time.

  • In the lesson editor:

    • Where it fits lists every free slot.
    • Fix it for me finds the best place, moving as little as possible, and shows the changes before making them.

  • Lock a lesson that must never move.

  • Auto build:

    • Give each group a weekly plan: how many lessons of each subject, double or single, which days and times.
    • Build the whole timetable then fills the gaps, improves what is there, or rebuilds from the plans. It runs in the background, keeps to the rules, and shows the result before anything changes. Undo puts it back.

  • Subject choices (under More; was Options) builds option blocks, where pupils split into the subjects they chose.

Two lessons in one period (halves)

A class can have two lessons in one period, one after the other: for example Reading, then Regulation. The bell times do not change.

  • In the lesson editor, Part of the period: All of it, First half or Second half.
  • Or drag one lesson onto another and choose One after the other. The lesson that was there becomes the first half.
  • To set several at once, select them and choose First half or Second half in the editor.

Two halves of a period never clash, even with the same staff, students and room. The Timetable grid shows 1st half and 2nd half. The staff Viewer shows First half and Second half, and the Lessons sheet of the workbook has a Half column.

Adding a lesson to an empty slot

Click the empty slot. The lesson editor shows up to three Suggested lessons: the subjects this class usually has at that time on other days (or, failing that, the ones it has most), each with the adults and room it usually has, if they are free then. Click one to add it, with the class's student list. Or pick any subject below the suggestions, as before.

A long lesson for one pupil, alongside the group

Click the first period, add the lesson (or click it if it is there), and set Length. Length goes up to the end of the day. If the later periods already have lessons, you are asked:

  • Keep both, for some pupils: it runs alongside the group's lessons and starts with nobody on it. Add the pupil (for example one pupil all day, periods 1 to 8).
  • Keep both, with the whole group: it runs alongside, with the same pupils.
  • Replace them: the lessons at those times go, as before. Undo brings them back.

If a pupil on it is also on another of the group's lessons at those times, the editor says so, with Take them off the other lessons.

Copying it to other days or slots (Copy this … to other slots, or Copy the selection to other days) asks what to do if a slot already has a lesson:

  • Replace it (as before)
  • Keep both: run alongside it
  • Keep both, and take these pupils off the other lesson there

A lesson for part of the group starts on the last one, so the group's lessons stay and the pupil moves. A whole-group lesson starts on Replace. Paste (Ctrl+V) still replaces; drag and drop asks.

Editing on People and rooms

You don't need to go to the whole Timetable to change one person's lessons. On People and rooms (Staff, Students or Rooms):

  • Click a lesson to open the lesson editor beside the grid. Changes are made there, on that page. The lesson is outlined on everyone who is in it.
  • Click an empty slot to add a lesson for that person or room:
    • Students: the lesson goes in the pupil's class with only that pupil on it, alongside whatever the class has then. Nothing of the class's is replaced. Pick a subject, then add staff and a room.
    • Staff and Rooms: choose the class first (the classes they are most often with come first), then a subject. They go on it as staff, or it goes in that room.
  • Right-click a lesson for the quick menu (Edit, Swap, Change room, Copy, Lock, Delete).
  • Open in Timetable in the editor shows the same slot in the whole Timetable.
  • Click a name for their whole timetable, as before.

Reading the grid

  • Hover over a lesson (or tab to it) for a card with all of it: subject, when, group, room, staff by name and students.
  • Compact or Comfortable (above the grid) sets how big each lesson is. Compact fits more on screen; Comfortable has bigger text. Your choice is remembered on that computer.
  • Colour key and tips (above the grid) opens the colour key and the tips for selecting, moving and copying.

The quick menu on a lesson

Right-click a lesson on the Timetable grid (or press Shift+F10 on the chosen lesson, or press and hold it on a touch screen) for: Edit, Swap or replace a member of staff, Change room, Copy to other slots, No staff needed, Lock or Unlock, and Delete. Undo works for all of them.

In the lesson editor, the Room list puts the rooms that are free at that time first, and says how many are free. Rooms in use say what is in them. The Add staff list already puts free people first.

Dragging lessons

Drag a lesson to move it; hold Ctrl (or Alt) to copy it.

  • To an empty slot: it moves. If it lands next to the same subject for the same pupils, you can also join the two into a double.
  • Onto another lesson: a menu asks what to do.
    • Swap places: the two lessons change places.
    • Merge into...: one lesson, the one already there, with both sets of staff and students.
    • One after the other: a half each.
    • Keep both at the same time: for a 1:1 or a split group beside it.
    • Replace: the lesson already there is deleted.
    • Cancel, or press Escape.
  • Onto another lesson in the same period (for example one of three 1:1s): Merge into makes them one lesson, or One after the other gives each half the period. To merge every lesson in a period, open one and choose Merge these ... into one; for many periods, select them and choose Merge each period into one lesson.
  • Into another class's row: a whole-class lesson takes that class's list.

Anything that breaks a rule asks first. Undo puts any of these back.

Classes with more than one lesson in a period

A class can have more than one lesson at once: a 1:1 beside the group, or the group split in two. When you change a whole week (click a class's name on the Timetable grid to select its week, or use Add to class team and all its lessons), the app keeps those lessons apart:

  • Someone added to all the lessons goes in one lesson per period: the main one, with most students. Nobody is put in two lessons at once, and the app says where the class was split.
  • Set to class list leaves pupils in their 1:1 or small group; the main lesson gets the rest.
  • Copying to other days copies every lesson in each period, and Join into one block joins each group of students with itself.

Adding someone to all of a class's lessons

In Setup, Staff, tick the person (or several people), choose the class in the list, then choose Add to class team and all its lessons. They go on every lesson of that class and join its team. Team only adds them to the team without changing lessons. In Auto build, each member of a class's Staff team shows how many of its lessons they are on, with Put on all its lessons. Take off all its lessons does the opposite. If they are already teaching somewhere else at one of those times, the app says how many clash; Checks lists them.

Taking someone off the timetable, or swapping them

The quickest way is Swap or replace a person. Open it from Home (Quick jobs), from One person (when it shows a member of staff), from a lesson's quick menu, or by searching (Ctrl K). Then:

  1. Who comes off. Type part of a name and choose them. From a lesson, its adults are listed.
  2. Which lessons. This lesson only, all of that class's lessons, or all their lessons.
  3. Who goes on instead. People who are free for every one of those lessons come first, marked Free. Others say how many they are busy for. Nobody just takes the person off.

Before you confirm, it says what will happen. If the new person is teaching or not working at some of those times, it says "3 of these clash", names them, and offers to leave those with the person coming off, so nothing clashes. Swapping all of a person's lessons also swaps their class team places. Undo puts it all back.

The older way still works, in Setup, Staff:

  • Take off every lesson: tick the person (or several), then choose it. They come off every lesson and every class team, but stay on the staff list. The app asks first.
  • Swap on all their lessons: tick the person leaving, choose who takes over in Swap for…, then choose it. The new person gets every one of their lessons and their class team places. If the new person is already teaching at some of those times, the app says how many clash; Checks lists them.

Both can be undone.

Blocked time: PPA, ECT time, meetings and more

Blocked time is part of a person's usual week that is not a lesson: PPA, ECT time, a Meeting, Maintenance, or Other.

  • No lesson is placed there. Auto build keeps lessons off it, the lesson editor and suggestions don't offer that person then, and a lesson already there is listed in Checks to move.
  • It never needs cover when they are away, and they are not asked to cover during it.
  • Their timetable shows the type (PPA, Meeting and so on) in the staff Viewer, on Staff day and on printouts. The note is only seen in the Builder.

To add it for several people at once, use Setup, Blocked time. Choose who (one or more people, or Everyone, All teachers, or All TAs and support staff), what it is, an optional note, and the days and periods. Then choose Block out this time. The list below shows all blocked time, with Remove.

To add it for one person, open People and rooms, Staff and click an empty slot. Choose Block out: PPA (or another type). Click a blocked slot to change its type, repeat it at that time every day, or remove it. Undo works for all of these.

In the Excel workbook, the Staff sheet has a Blocked time column, for example Wed P6 Meeting (Phase meeting); Fri P2 PPA.

Students with no staff

Checks flags any lesson with students on it and no staff, and the Timetable grid marks it red. If the lesson does not need staff (college, work experience, an off-site placement), tick No staff needed for this lesson in the lesson editor, or No staff needed for any ... lesson for every lesson of that subject. Checks has the same two buttons by each lesson, and Setup, Subjects has a No staff needed column.

Tidying names (one name for one thing)

When the same thing has several names, reports and searches split it up.

  • Setup, Subjects shows Names that look like the same thing. It finds every alternative provision name (Alt Provision, Alternative Provision, AP - ...), offsite names, reduced timetable names, 1:1 names, and names that differ only in capitals or punctuation. Untick any that are something different, check the name in Merge as and the start of the titles in Titles start with (AP: or Offsite: to begin with), then choose Merge the ticked ones. Each lesson keeps which provision it is in its title, for example AP - Player Ready becomes Alternative provision, titled AP: Player Ready.
  • To merge any subjects yourself, tick them in the Subjects list, type the name, and choose Merge into one. Keep old names as lesson titles (the default) keeps the detail, after whatever you type in Titles start with: with AP: typed, AP - Player Ready becomes Alternative provision, titled AP: Player Ready. Generic names (Alt Provision, Off Site) get no title, as they say nothing extra.
  • Setup, Rooms has Merge into one too, for example Offsite_RSPCA into Offsite Provision.
  • In Reports, Who is doing what, Change all of these lessons gives every lesson the search found one subject or one title.

Everything that used the old names follows: lessons, plans, options, specialisms and iSAMS codes. Undo puts it back.

Who is doing what (Reports)

At the top of Reports, type what to look for, or choose a button: Reduced timetable, Alternative provision, Off site, 1:1, College or Not on site. It searches every lesson's subject, title, room and group in the usual week, and lists:

  • each student on those lessons, their group, how many periods a week and when
  • each member of staff, how many periods a week and when
  • every matching lesson (open Every lesson)

Download as a spreadsheet (CSV) saves the lessons with their staff and students. Words match loosely: "alternate provision", "Alt Provision" and "AP - Equine" are all found by Alternative provision, and "Reduced TT" by Reduced timetable. The report holds pupils' names, so keep printouts and downloads secure.

More than one site

For a school on several sites, give each room its Site in Setup, Rooms, and set Minutes to move between sites (in the Checks window, or on the School sheet of the workbook). Then:

  • Timetable puts each site's classes together under a heading with the site's name and colour.
    • The Site menu on the toolbar shows one site at a time. Clicking a site heading does the same; clicking it again shows all sites.
    • Untick Group by site for the usual order; each row then says its site under the class name.
    • A lesson in a room on another site from its class shows that site's name, such as South.
  • A class is based on the site of the rooms most of its lessons are in. To set it yourself, type it in the Site column of Setup, Classes and groups, or on the Groups sheet of the workbook.
  • Rooms shows each room's site under its name.
  • The lesson editor's room list names the site of any room on another site.
  • The rule not enough time to move between sites still flags staff and pupils who would have to move between sites without enough time.

A school on one site sees none of this.

Printing a timetable for one person

Open More, then One person. Choose staff or students and the look, then Print (this person) or Print all. Each person's timetable prints on one A4 landscape page. If a week is very full, it is printed slightly smaller, only as much as it needs; nothing is left out. The visual Day look still prints a page for each day.

Show: Staff, Pupils (Standard look) chooses whether each lesson lists the staff and the pupils on it. Staff are shown and pupils hidden to begin with. On a student's timetable, ticking Pupils lists the other pupils in each lesson, and the page warns that this copy is not for giving to the pupil. Printing follows your choice, and it is remembered on this computer. The visual looks have their own choices.

Site (for a school on more than one site) shows, and prints with Print all, only the groups, rooms, staff or pupils on that site. Someone who works on two sites is on both.

Printing the whole timetable

Print the whole timetable… (on One person, on Reports, or Ctrl K) prints one sheet with a row for each group, member of staff or room, and every period of the week across the page, like a master timetable.

  • One row for each: group, member of staff, or room.
  • Site: all sites, Each site on its own pages, or one site.
  • In each lesson: staff codes, room, and subject colours, each on or off.
  • Paper: A4 or A3 landscape (it suggests A3 when there are more than 30 periods a week). For A3, choose A3 in the printer settings too.

Rows carry on to the next page, with the days and periods repeated at the top. A key at the end explains the subject and staff codes. For staff, blocked time shows its type (PPA, Meeting) and times they don't work show Off. Your choices are remembered on this computer.

Order sorts the list, and Print all, by surname (the default) or first name, A to Z, or as in the list. Your choice is remembered on this computer.

Checks and the quality score

The Checks button, or the status bar, opens the Checks window.

Rules broken must be put right:

  • someone in two places at once
  • staff timetabled when they do not work
  • a room that is too small, or the wrong kind of room
  • a group short of adults for its ratio
  • pupils who must be kept apart taught together
  • not enough time to move between sites

The quality score (out of 100) shows how well the timetable meets the school's preferences. You choose how much each part counts:

  • subjects spread across the week
  • core subjects in the morning
  • no empty periods in a group's day
  • no long gaps in a teacher's day
  • few room changes
  • difficult slots shared fairly
  • key adults with their pupils

Snapshots and scenarios

  • Snapshots (File menu) keep a copy of the lessons to compare with later or go back to. One is taken automatically before big changes.
  • What if (was Scenarios) keeps scenarios: full working copies of the timetable, for trying ideas such as setting Year 9, or a teacher going part-time:
    • Open one, and every planning tool works on the copy. The real timetable, cover and the day-to-day pages are not affected.
    • Compare up to three scenarios with the real timetable, then Use as the real timetable. Undo works.

Insights

Insights shows:

  • the headline numbers
  • each person's workload, against the most they should teach
  • how many staff are free to cover in each period
  • rooms in use
  • groups against their weekly plans
  • pupils with no lesson
  • whether cover is shared fairly this term

Each part downloads as a spreadsheet.

Print the meeting pack (on Reports, in Home's Quick jobs, or through Ctrl K) prints one report for an SLT meeting in a single click:

  1. Staffing load: each person's periods a week, free periods, longest run and days with no break
  2. Cover: how many each person has covered this term, against the average
  3. Room use: how much of the week each room is used
  4. Problems: clashes, rules broken, warnings and lessons still to place

It names staff only; there is no pupil data in it.

Running the day

  • Cover:

    • Mark who is off: a whole day, part of a day, or several days.

    • The buttons along the top:

      • Cover this day chooses cover for every lesson that needs it on the day shown. Cover already chosen stays.
      • Print or copy has Print who is covering, Copy who is covering, Print cover slips and Print full cover sheets.
      • Several days, redo or clear opens the date range with Cover these days, Redo automatic cover and Clear cover choices (which asks first).

    • Automatic cover chooses fairly, in this order:

      1. people called in
      2. people who can take a colleague's whole day
      3. people on call
      4. whoever has covered least

      It keeps to each person's cover limit for the term, and roles marked last resort are only used when nobody else is free.

    • Add work for each covered lesson.

    • Print slips, or copy the list.

  • This week on Cover shows each day at a glance. Click a day to open it.
  • Today has the safety checks (DSLs, first aiders and senior staff in), pupils who are off, and the daily briefing.
  • Staff day shows each person's day, including cover. The staff view (File menu) shows the same for everyone, with nothing to edit.
  • Rest days and on call (under More; was Rota) handles rest days and on-call lists. Transport books vehicles and drivers.

Telling people

The bell counts staff with changes they have not been told about:

  • new or cancelled cover and duties
  • being called in
  • changes to their weekly timetable

For each person there is a ready-written message. You can:

  • send it by Email or Teams chat from your own account
  • Copy it
  • download a Calendar file
  • Put it in the outbox

Then mark the person as told. In the Power Apps edition, a flow your IT team sets up can send the outbox, and the bell shows what was sent.

A summary of changes for all staff

At the top of the bell's window, Summary of changes for all staff is one short list of what has changed in the usual week, by group, ready to send to everyone:

  • new, moved and removed lessons
  • new rooms and changes of staff
  • PPA, meetings and other blocked time, by type (never the note)

It has no pupil names, no cover and no reasons for absence.

  1. The first time, choose Start from now. Changes after that are listed.
  2. Type when the changes start, if you like (for example Monday 23 November). It goes in the heading.
  3. Choose Copy summary and paste it into an email or a Teams post, or Email it to open an email, or Print.
  4. Choose Mark as sent. The next summary lists only what changes after this. Undo works.

Changes since can also compare with any snapshot (File, Snapshots).

The bell's own messages to each person still work as before.

Lesson registers (Power Apps)

In the Power Apps staff app, staff choose Take register on a lesson they teach or cover today. They mark each pupil Present, Late, Absent or Out of lesson, then choose Save register. Pupils the office has reported off are already marked Absent.

In the Timetable Builder, Today, the Lesson registers card shows who has taken each register and which are missing. It can be downloaded as a spreadsheet.

The staff Viewer

Setup, then Staff viewer (or File, then Publish for staff) makes a separate, read-only page for staff. On a phone it opens on Today:

  • today's changes (cover they are doing, rooms that have changed)
  • Now and Next all day long: what is first before school, the lesson now with Room, Group and the other adults and the minutes left, the break, and what tomorrow starts with after school
  • a search for a colleague, room or group
  • the whole day, each lesson labelled

The other tabs are Week, Find (staff, rooms, groups, and students if allowed) and Cover. Display and settings has text size and an easier-to-read font.

Tick what staff may see, then download one of these:

  • the Viewer for all staff: one file, where each person picks their name once
  • a Viewer for each person: a zip, with one file each holding only that person's days
  • the published copy: timetable-published.json, for a Viewer on the school's web site

Publish again when the timetable or cover changes. The Viewer never shows pupils, emails, notes or reasons for absence. STAFF-VIEWER.md explains how to get it onto staff phones.

The pupil Viewer

Setup, then Pupil viewer makes a very simple page for one pupil: what is on now and next with a symbol, the room and their adult, a countdown, and a gentle note when a day is different. Download one pupil's file, or a zip with one file for every pupil. Each file holds only that pupil's own timetable, with no other pupil's name in it. You can add photos of adults if you wish. PUPIL-VIEWER.md has the details.

A new school year

File, then New school year… (or Ctrl K, "new school year") makes next year's timetable from a copy of this one, in four steps:

  1. What to keep. Staff, rooms, subjects, groups and class lists, the school day and rules are kept. Choose the lessons: keep this year's as a starting point, keep only locked lessons, or start empty. Absences and cover are not copied. Term dates are not copied unless you tick it.
  2. Year groups. Every pupil moves up a year (Reception to Year 1, Year 7 to Year 8). Choose which year leaves; the leavers are named, and are taken off class lists and lessons in the new year.
  3. Name. The name moves on a year (2026 to 2027 becomes 2027 to 2028), and you set the first day.
  4. Check and make.
    • Download the new year as a file gives you next year's file and leaves this timetable as it is.
    • Switch to it now asks first. It then takes a snapshot of this year's lessons and downloads a backup of the whole timetable before switching. Undo goes straight back.

Where the timetable is kept, and saving

Open File, then Where your data is kept, or click the chip by Save. The timetable can be kept in:

  • this file
  • an Excel workbook
  • a folder of CSV files
  • Excel in OneDrive or SharePoint (Microsoft 365)
  • Google Sheets
  • Dataverse, in the Power Apps edition

Save writes to wherever it is kept:

  • In Microsoft 365 or Google Sheets, if someone else saved since you opened it, you choose whether to open their version or save yours over it. A copy of yours is downloaded first.
  • In the Power Apps edition, changes save by themselves.

Save a backup file (File menu) every week or two, and keep it in the school's own storage.

Who can change it

Setup, then Who can change this timetable:

  • Can change everything.
  • Cover and the day only: Staff day, Today, Cover, Rest days and on call, and Transport.
  • Can look, not change.

People are known by the account they sign in with: Microsoft 365, Google or Power Apps. You cannot remove your own access by mistake.

The school's look

Setup, then Your school's look: a logo for the menu, the staff view and printouts, and a colour. The colour is adjusted if needed, so text stays easy to read.

Personal data

Setup, then Personal data:

  • Everything about one person downloads all the timetable holds about a member of staff or a pupil, for a subject access request.
  • Remove old records deletes day-to-day records from before a date, after a backup. Agree how long to keep them with your data protection officer.

The AI assistant (optional)

The AI assistant is off unless someone who can change everything turns it on, in Setup, then AI assistant. Your IT team chooses the AI service and sets it up (see AI-PROVIDERS.md). Only people who can change everything see the AI buttons.

What it can do. Look for the buttons marked with a spark:

Button Where What it does
Fix all clashes Timetable, the toolbar Moves as few lessons as possible to clear the rule problems
Timetable this class Auto build, each group Places the lessons a group is missing from its weekly plan
Find cover Cover, each person off that day Chooses who covers each lesson, fairly
Balance this week One person, a member of staff Spreads one person's lessons more evenly
Explain this placement The lesson editor Says why a lesson is where it is, and whether a better slot exists
Run a what-if What if, with a scenario open Tries an idea you type, in the scenario only
Map my spreadsheet Upload sheets Matches a lessons sheet's columns when the app does not recognise them

How it works.

  1. The AI only ever sees codes, never names. You can choose to see exactly what is sent first.
  2. The AI proposes. The app checks the proposal against its own rules. If anything fails, it tells the AI why and asks again, up to five times. If it still fails, nothing changes.
  3. You see before and after, with each change listed. Choose Approve, Edit (untick the changes you do not want; the rest is checked again) or Reject.
  4. Approving is one change, so Undo puts it back.

The AI never changes the timetable by itself. Locked lessons never move, an action for one group or person cannot touch anyone else's lessons, and a what-if only changes the open scenario.

In a what-if, choose people, groups, rooms and subjects from the Insert a person, group, room or subject list rather than typing their names. A typed name is not sent; the app asks you to use the list.

Making your own AI buttons

Setup, then AI assistant lists where every AI button is, with Go there for each. Setup, then AI actions lists every AI button.

  • The seven official ones are locked. Untick On to hide one, View to see exactly how it works, or Duplicate to make your own version.
  • New action opens a form: a name and button label, an icon, what it does (move lessons, choose cover, explain, or match spreadsheet columns), where the button goes, what data it may send, and the prompt. Insert placeholders such as {{group_code}} from the list. Problems are listed as you type, and it cannot be saved until they are put right.
  • Preview what it sends shows the request, built from a made-up school; nothing is sent.
